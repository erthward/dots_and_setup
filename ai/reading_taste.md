# reading taste (distilled by Claude Code from my Zotero library, 2026-11-19)

Source: ~5,400 items (~4,800 journal articles), the ❤️‍🔥 collection (my 32 all-time favorites), and my
notes/annotations. Complements `research_profile.md` (what I do); this file is about what I read and
what I find exciting. Citation rule still applies: cite only from `~/Zotero/deth_library.bib`.

## diet at a glance
- **venues**: Nature (~650), Science (~340), PNAS (~245) dominate, then GCB, Mol Ecol, RSE, Nat Ecol Evol,
  Nat Commun, Nat Clim Change, Ecol Lett, TREE, Evolution, GEB, Am Nat, Proc B, MEE, Agric For Meteorol,
  Annu Rev Ecol Evol Syst, J Biogeogr, Ecography, New Phytol, Biotropica. Also atmospheric/hydrology
  journals (GRL, JGR-Biogeosciences, J Climate/JAMC, WRR, Int J Climatol).
- **intake mode**: heavy table-of-contents skimming (GCB, Mol Ecol, Ecol Lett, GEB, J Biogeogr, DDI, Evol
  Appl, Ecol Evol, Austral Ecology, Biotropica, AGU journals) plus Nature/Science news, careers, and
  opinion pieces. Most items are saved to remember they exist, not because I've read them closely.
- **era**: skewed recent (~57% published 2020+), but anchored by deep classics I treat as foundational:
  Wright (1931, 1943), Kimura/Ohta (nearly neutral theory), Slatkin (clines, gene flow), Levins,
  MacArthur, Margalef, Pielou, Geiger's *Climate Near the Ground*, Humboldt's *Ansichten der Natur*,
  Janzen 1967, Frankie/Opler/Borchert-era tropical phenology.

## core topic clusters (roughly by volume)
1. **climate + biogeography of the tropics**: tropical forests, mountains, montane cloud forests,
   Amazon, Andes, Wet Tropics; latitudinal diversity gradients; cradles/museums; "mountain passes" lineage.
2. **phenology at every scale**: LSP, tropical phenology (light vs water control, leaf flush, bimodal
   regimes), marine/phytoplankton phenology, bird breeding/migration timing, allochrony and isolation
   by time, herbarium/citizen-science phenology, seasonality theory.
3. **remote sensing of vegetation function**: SIF (~140 titles), NIRv, GPP, OCO-2/TROPOMI, MODIS,
   flux towers, cloud/fog remote sensing, terrain-corrected indices.
4. **atmosphere/hydroclimate as biological driver**: monsoons, ITCZ, bimodal precipitation, orographic
   rain, moisture recycling, low-level jets, fog and low cloud, topoclimate, microclimate/microrefugia.
5. **population/landscape genomics**: polygenic adaptation and genetic architecture, migration-selection
   balance, clines, genomic offset (with healthy skepticism), landscape resistance methods, IBD/IBE,
   spatiotemporal popgen, ARGs, macrogenetics, genetic diversity indicators.
6. **speciation and macroevolution**: allochronic speciation, ecological speciation, diversification
   rates, eco-evolutionary simulation of diversification over paleo-landscapes (gen3sis-type models).
7. **applied/climate solutions**: agroforestry and NCS (carbon, soil C, trees outside forests),
   REDD+/offset integrity, koala ecology and management, SDMs and data integration, conservation genetics.
8. **wide curiosity outside the field**: neuroscience/cognition, human evolution and ancient DNA,
   horizontal gene transfer, AI/LLMs (and their effects on science), science policy/funding, Global
   South equity in science, Indigenous knowledge, autism, meditation/psychedelics, deep-time Earth history.

## what my favorite papers (❤️‍🔥) have in common
Read these as the template for "a paper I'll love":
- **a simple physical mechanism generating a big biological pattern**: Janzen 1967 (thermal
  seasonality → mountain passes "higher"); Wright & van Schaik 1994 (irradiance → tropical phenology);
  van Schaik et al. 1993; Martin et al. 2009 (asynchrony of seasons → tropical differentiation);
  Killeen et al. 2007 (winds + topography → Andean wet/dry spots); Dobrowski 2011 (terrain → microrefugia).
- **earth-process + evolution simulation over deep time**: Rangel et al. 2018; Skeels et al. 2023
  (Wallace's Line); Marder et al. 2025 (uplift → biodiversity); Kuhnhäuser et al. 2025 (island
  geography → rattans); Malanoski et al. 2025; Zeller & Timmermann 2024 (human niche in 3D terrain).
- **clouds, fog, and water as overlooked niche axes**: Welch et al. 2008 and Nair et al. 2008 (TMCF
  cloud immersion from MODIS); Wilson & Jetz 2016; Torregrosa et al. 2015 (GOES fog); McLaughlin et al.
  2017 (hydrologic refugia); Crimmins et al. 2011 (water balance → *downhill* shifts).
- **conceptual clarification with operational definitions**: Holt 2009 and Soberón & Nakamura 2009
  (niche concepts); Peterson et al. 2011; Bradburd & Ralph 2019 (spatial popgen: "it's about time");
  Coyne & Orr 2004.
- **minimal stochastic/demographic models that explain a universal pattern**: van Nes et al. 2024
  (rarity as a sticky state); Jops & O'Dwyer 2023 (life-history complementarity); Tuljapurkar et al. 2021.
- **paradigm-breaking surprises**: Belkin et al. 2019 (double descent vs bias-variance); Walsh et al.
  2023 (First Peoples' knowledge resolves fairy circles); Crimmins et al. 2011.
- **global synthesis maps of timing**: Boyce et al. 2017 (marine plankton phenology); Yang et al. 2021;
  Jahn et al. 2020 (Neotropical bird migration); my own Terasaki Hart et al. 2025.
Common thread: **space × time × mechanism**, preferably at global scale, preferably tropical, with
an elegant explanation and a figure that makes the pattern visible.

## how I read (from my notes and annotations)
- **critically, at the level of definitions and caveats**: e.g., on Cabon et al. 2022 I unpacked a
  terminology clash (physiological vs ecosystem "source/sink"), flagged the lack of tropical data,
  and asked where unallocated carbon goes (root exudation not considered).
- **always mapping papers onto my own data**: e.g., Boos & Pascale 2021's monsoon MSE footprint lining
  up with the spring/summer photosynthesis split in my maps near Punta Eugenia; questioning my Florida
  mangrove interpretation against Jiang et al. 2025.
- **generating hypotheses in the margins**: shifting climate timings creating new AxA landscapes
  (Bede-Fazekas et al. 2024); fungus → orchid HGT implying huitlacoche → maize HGT is worth testing
  (Sinn & Barrett 2020); aspect-driven leaf-fall *timing* being overlooked in cloud forests (Chang et al.
  2026); inverted fog seasonality in cloud forest vs montane ag (Chiu et al. 2026).
- **delighted by first evidence for neglected drivers**: e.g., minimum temperature as a tropical
  phenology cue (Bendix et al. 2006).
- **theory-grounded methods critique**: e.g., annotations on landscape-genomics sampling that invoke
  Slatkin's characteristic cline length to argue paired designs can sit inside a single cline;
  a methods-skeptic shelf on Mantel tests, resistance-surface optimization, and genomic offset.

## how to use this (instructions for Claude)
- **assume fluency** in the clusters above and the classics; skip textbook explanations of LSP, SIF,
  harmonic regression, IBD/IBE, genomic offset, niche concepts, latitudinal gradients, etc.
- **when suggesting literature**: favor mechanism-rich, global-scale, tropical, or cross-disciplinary
  work (atmospheric science, hydrology, geomorphology, paleoclimate ↔ ecology/evolution). Flag
  counterevidence and methods critiques, not just supporting papers. Check whether it's already in my
  library before presenting it as new; if it isn't in the .bib, say so and mark [CITE].
- **connect to the geography of timing**: when a paper touches seasonality, cloud/fog, topography,
  or synchrony, point out the angle for asynchrony/AxA/phenocycles, even if the paper doesn't.
- **raise the bar for "interesting"**: a surprising mechanism, a counterintuitive result, or a clean
  conceptual reframing beats incremental, single-site, or purely correlational work.
- **don't overweight my off-field saves** (news, AI, neuro, policy): they reflect curiosity, not expertise. However, in principle, this sort of reading/skimming creates ongoing potential for cool, productive cross-disciplinary blue-sky thinking. Recommendations that fall into this basket are welcome as a sort of by-the-way/postscript treat.
